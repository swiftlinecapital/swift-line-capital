Building and Maintaining a Strong Business Credit Score

Published: April 12, 2026 | By Swift Line Capital
Your business credit score is distinct from personal credit and plays a critical role in your ability to access financing. Understanding how business credit works and actively building your credit profile opens doors to better rates and larger loan amounts. Unlike personal credit scores, which range 300-850, business credit scores vary by agency. Dun & Bradstreet (DUNS) scores range 0-100, with higher being better. Experian business credit uses a 0-100 scale, and Equifax uses similar metrics. Establishing business credit begins with separating your business from personal finances. Obtain an EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS. Open a business bank account in the business name. Register your business with the Secretary of State. Building positive credit history requires establishing business accounts with vendors. Open accounts with office supply companies, shipping companies, or wholesalers that report to business credit bureaus. Use these accounts, pay on time, and build payment history. Maintain business credit by paying all obligations on time. Late payments significantly damage business credit. Keep business finances clean and organized. Maintain current business licenses and registrations. File business taxes on time. Monitor your business credit reports regularly through Dun & Bradstreet, Experian, and Equifax. Dispute any inaccuracies. Accurate credit reporting is essential for favorable financing. A strong business credit score can result in better interest rates, higher borrowing limits, and more favorable terms across all financing options. Swift Line Capital evaluates business credit as part of our underwriting, and we work with businesses at various credit stages.
