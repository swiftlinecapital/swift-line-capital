The Construction Loan Process: From Approval to Completion

Published: April 10, 2026 | By Swift Line Capital
Construction loans are specialized financing designed for building projects. Unlike traditional mortgages that provide a lump sum, construction loans are drawn as construction progresses through a process called 'draws'. Before construction begins, you need a detailed project plan. This includes architectural drawings, contractor estimates, construction schedule, and engineering plans. Lenders review these to understand project scope, timeline, and cost. Construction loans typically have two phases. The construction phase is interest-only, with payments based on the outstanding balance. After construction completes, the loan converts to permanent financing (typically a conventional mortgage) with principal and interest payments. Draw requests are submitted as construction progresses. After the foundation is complete, you submit a draw request with photos and contractor verification. The lender inspects and disburses funds (typically 80-90% of completed work value). This process repeats through framing, roof, interior finishing, and completion. Lenders protect themselves by having independent inspectors verify work completion before disbursing funds. This ensures the project is progressing as planned and that lender funds are being used appropriately. Timelines matter significantly in construction lending. Projects that lag behind schedule result in extended holding periods with higher interest costs. Projects that exceed budget might not have sufficient financing to complete. Common construction loan requirements include 15-25% down payment, strong contractor relationships and experience, detailed project planning, and realistic timelines.
