Hard Money vs Conventional Loans: Which is Right for Your Deal?

Published: April 25, 2026 | By Swift Line Capital
Real estate investors often face a choice between hard money loans and conventional financing. Each has distinct advantages and disadvantages, and the right choice depends on your specific deal, timeline, and financial situation. The Fundamentals Conventional real estate loans come from traditional banks and credit unions. They typically require strong credit scores (680+), significant down payments (15-25%), W-2 income verification, and property appraisals. The approval process takes 30-45 days but results in lower interest rates (4-7%) and longer amortization periods (15-30 years). Hard money loans come from private lenders and investor groups. They focus on the property's value and potential rather than the borrower's credit or income. Approval is fast (24-48 hours) but interest rates are higher (8-15%) and terms are shorter (1-5 years). Speed and Flexibility The most compelling advantage of hard money is speed. When you find a motivated seller or opportunity property that won't be available next month, fast capital becomes critical. Hard money lenders focus on the deal fundamentals and can approve in hours rather than the weeks required for conventional lending. Conventional loans are slower but provide the cost-of-capital advantage of lower interest rates. If you have time to secure financing, conventional loans significantly reduce your long-term carrying costs. Credit and Income Requirements Conventional loans focus heavily on borrower creditworthiness and income verification. This creates challenges for real estate investors whose income is primarily from rental properties rather than W-2 employment, or those with credit challenges due to business fluctuations. Hard money lenders evaluate the deal rather than the borrower. A powerful investor with poor credit can access hard money, as can a newer investor without extensive real estate history. This flexibility is invaluable for investors operating outside conventional lending parameters. Loan-to-Value Considerations Conventional lenders typically finance up to 80% of property value, requiring 20% down. This limits their utility for investors without significant capital reserves or for deals with tighter margins. Hard money lenders often finance 70-85% of after-repair value (ARV) for fix & flip deals or purchase price for other scenarios. While this may sound lower, it's often higher in dollar terms because it's calculated on the higher ARV value in fix & flip scenarios. Amortization and Payments Conventional loans offer 15-30 year amortizations, resulting in manageable monthly payments. Interest-only options exist but are less common and more expensive. Hard money loans are typically short-term (1-3 years) and often structured as interest-only payments during the loan period with principal due at maturity. This preserves cash flow during the project timeline but requires a clear exit strategy (sell property or refinance to conventional). When to Choose Hard Money Hard money is optimal for fix & flip projects with quick timelines, deals requiring fast closing, situations where your financial profile doesn't fit conventional lending criteria, bridge financing gaps with conventional loans, or investor portfolios that need capital velocity rather than minimal cost. When to Choose Conventional Conventional loans make sense for long-term buy-and-hold rentals, your financial profile fits conventional underwriting, you prioritize minimizing long-term interest costs, or you have the time to wait for conventional approval. The Strategic Approach Many experienced investors use both. They use hard money for acquisitions and quick repositioning, then refinance to conventional financing once the property is stabilized.
