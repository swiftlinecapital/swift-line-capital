Comprehensive SBA Loan Guide for Small Business Owners

Published: April 8, 2026 | By Swift Line Capital
Small Business Administration (SBA) loans are government-backed financing programs designed to help small businesses access capital. These programs reduce risk for lenders by having the federal government guarantee a portion of the loan, allowing lenders to offer more favorable terms. The most common SBA loan program is the 7(a) Loan Program, which guarantees up to 90% of the loan amount (up to $5 million) for qualifying small businesses. This government guarantee means lenders can offer longer terms and lower down payments than conventional financing. SBA 7(a) loans can be used for working capital, equipment purchases, real estate acquisition, business expansion, or refinancing existing business debt. The flexibility in uses makes them valuable for many business scenarios. Qualifying for SBA loans requires demonstrating that your business is small (under $7.5 million annual revenue for most industries), that you will use the loan personally in the business, that you have a reasonable ability to repay, and that the business is for-profit. SBA loans typically carry interest rates of prime plus 2.75% for loans under $25,000, with higher rates for larger loans. For comparison, conventional business loans typically charge 3-4% above prime. The government guarantee allows these favorable rates. Terms are typically longer than conventional financing. Working capital loans might be 7 years, while equipment loans match the equipment life (up to 25 years), and real estate loans are 25-30 years. The SBA application process is more involved than conventional financing. You'll need a detailed business plan, 2 years of personal and business tax returns, current financial statements, and business history. Processing takes 5-7 weeks typically.
