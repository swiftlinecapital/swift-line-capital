Working Capital Options: Keep Your Business Operating Smoothly

Published: April 5, 2026 | By Swift Line Capital
Working capital is the cash needed for day-to-day business operations. For many businesses, cash flow timing creates challenges. You might purchase inventory in bulk before selling it, have seasonal revenue fluctuations, or need to bridge gaps between paying employees and receiving customer payments. Working capital financing provides quick access to cash to manage these timing differences. Rather than depleting your reserves or stalling growth, working capital loans give you the cash flow flexibility to operate optimally. Common working capital needs include inventory financing for businesses carrying significant inventory. Accounts receivable financing (factoring) where you borrow against outstanding customer invoices. Seasonal financing for businesses with revenue fluctuations throughout the year. Bridge financing to cover gaps between business milestones. Working capital loans are typically short-term (6-24 months) and unsecured or lightly secured. Terms are more flexible than traditional term loans, recognizing that working capital needs are temporary. Rates for working capital loans typically range 6-18% annually depending on lender and loan structure. Factor in the short terms means you pay interest for 6-24 months rather than 5-7 years of longer-term debt. Application is typically faster than conventional loans. Many lenders approve working capital loans within 24-48 hours based on recent business financials and personal credit. The key to working capital financing is clear cash flow analysis. Lenders want to understand your cash flow cycle, when you have cash inflows and outflows, and how the working capital loan fits into this cycle.
